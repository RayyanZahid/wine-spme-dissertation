# Cabernet Sauvignon Aroma Chemistry — Compound Brief

**Specialist 2/6 deliverable** — dissertation: "Method Development, MRM Optimization, and Validation of SPME Arrow Headspace GC-MS/MS for the Quantitative Profiling of Aroma-Active Volatiles in Cabernet Sauvignon Wine."

Retrieval window: 2026-05-12. All concentration / threshold values are cited inline. Where two sources disagree, both ranges are reported.

A note on units: this brief consistently reports concentration and threshold in the unit historically used for each class — methoxypyrazines and thiols in **ng/L**; norisoprenoids, terpenes, volatile phenols and most fermentation esters in **µg/L**; higher alcohols and acetic acid in **mg/L**. OAVs are unitless (concentration / threshold, same units). Where the literature used a different unit, the value is converted.

A note on matrix-dependence: thresholds in red wine matrix differ materially from water or model wine. Where a red-wine threshold is published it is reported preferentially; where only a water / hydroalcoholic value exists this is flagged ("WATER" or "MODEL WINE"). The dissertation should report all OAVs using a single, stated matrix (most papers use the lowest published wine-matrix value).

## 1. Methoxypyrazines (varietal — "green pepper" signature)

The methoxypyrazines are the chemical signature of the Cabernet family (Cabernet Sauvignon, Cabernet Franc, Merlot, Carménère, Sauvignon Blanc). They are grape-derived, NOT formed during fermentation. They are the most potent volatiles found in wine on a per-ng basis; IPMP is detectable in water below 1 ng/L.

## 5. Varietal Thiols (yeast-released — "tropical fruit")

3MH, 3MHA, and 4MMP are cysteinylated/glutathione-bound in grape must and released by yeast during fermentation. Cab Sauv is a *minor* thiol cultivar relative to Sauvignon Blanc, but quantification still matters — only ~3.2 % of Cys-3MH is released in Cab Sauv vs Merlot ([Tominaga, Dubourdieu 2006 — cited in labexcell review](https://labexcell.com/en/aromas/volatile-thiols)).

## Open items / caveats for the dissertation

- The Polášková 2008 review (RSC Chem Soc Rev) and the Waterhouse UC Davis "What's in Wine" pages were paywalled / blocked during this research pass; the dissertation should retrieve the original review in print or via UC institutional access for its definitive threshold tables.
- TPB threshold (0.04 µg/L) is sourced from Slaghenaufi 2018 (Valpolicella) — Cab Sauv-specific replication is desirable but not yet published. Cite Slaghenaufi for the threshold, note the inference.
- For the smoke-taint sub-panel, **glycoconjugate quantification** (LC-MS, not GC) complements the free-VP MRM and is now considered necessary for forensic smoke-exposure attribution per AWRI 2024 guidance.
- The varietal thiols (3MH, 3MHA, 4MMP) require **in-situ derivatization** (e.g., pentafluorobenzyl bromide or 4,4'-dithiodipyridine) to be tractable by HS-SPME Arrow — note this in method limitations if thiols are included in the MRM panel.
- IBMP threshold ranges in the literature span 1 → 16 ng/L depending on matrix and panel selection. Sacks (Cornell) and Allen-Lacey (CSIRO) reported different anchor values. Dissertation should pick a single value (e.g., 15 ng/L red wine, Allen 1991 / commonly cited) and footnote the range.
